Where these slogans belong

Hygiene is a matter of habit, and habits are formed at particular places: the wash basin, the canteen door, the water cooler, the tiffin table, the playground tap. Put one slogan at each place, about the habit that belongs there. A whole chart of health rules on the classroom wall is read once. A single line at the tap is read before every meal.

Write your own

Keep to simple, everyday habits that every textbook agrees on: wash hands with soap before eating and after the toilet, cover your mouth when coughing, drink clean water, keep nails short, eat fresh food, sleep on time, play outside. Turn the habit into a rhyme or a rule of three. Children remember "Wet, soap, rinse" far better than a sentence.

  • English pairs: clean and seen, soap and hope, wash and dash, germ and firm, health and wealth.
  • Hindi pairs: सफ़ाई and दवाई, हाथ and साथ, स्वस्थ and मस्त, धोओ and खोओ, आदत and सेहत.

A note on what slogans cannot say

These slogans are general good-habit reminders. A slogan must not promise that a habit prevents or cures any disease, name medicines, or give advice on illness. For anything about sickness, vaccines or treatment, the school nurse, a doctor or the health department's own posters are the right source.

Ideas for a health week

  • A handwashing song of your own, sung at the taps before lunch.
  • A "clean nails Monday" check done by class monitors, with stars and no scolding.
  • A tiffin day when everyone brings one fruit.

Using these slogans

  • Place the slogan where the habit happens: at the tap, the canteen door, the water cooler.
