How to write a slogan, and make a winning poster

A slogan is the shortest speech you will ever write. It has to be understood in one reading, remembered after one hearing and easy to say by a crowd. Here is how to write one, and how to put it on a poster so that it wins the competition.

Five rules of a good slogan

  1. Short. Aim for three to eight words. If it does not fit on one line of a chart, cut it.
  2. One message. "Save water" or "plant trees", not both.
  3. Sound. Rhyme, rhythm or a repeated letter makes a line stick: "नल खुला मत छोड़ो, पानी से नाता जोड़ो".
  4. An action. Tell people what to do: close, plant, wear, vote, wash.
  5. Positive where possible. "Helmet on, worry gone" is remembered longer than a threat.

Write yours in five minutes

  1. Write the message as a plain sentence: "People should not waste electricity."
  2. Circle the action word: waste, save, switch off.
  3. Find a rhyme or a partner word for it: off and on, bachao and ghatao.
  4. Write three versions. Say each aloud.
  5. Keep the one a seven year old could repeat after hearing it once.

Poster tips for slogan competitions

  • Slogan first. Give it the top third of the chart, in the biggest letters. Judges read from across the room.
  • One picture. A single clear drawing beats six small ones.
  • Two or three colours. Dark letters on a light chart are easiest to read.
  • Guidelines in pencil. Rule light lines before lettering, and rub them out after.
  • Check the spelling of every word, twice, especially matras in Hindi and Punjabi.
  • Name, class and section go small, at the bottom right.

For rallies and prabhat pheris

  • Choose call and response lines: the leader shouts the first half, everyone answers with the second.
  • Practise with a clap on each beat so that the whole class keeps time.
  • Keep five or six slogans and repeat them. Too many, and nobody remembers any.

What to avoid

  • Slogans that insult a group, a religion, a region or a person.
  • Party names, candidates or symbols in school and voter awareness work.
  • Health claims you cannot support. "Wash hands" is fine. "Cures disease" is not.
  • Copying a brand's advertising line for your own shop.
