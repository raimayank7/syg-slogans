Shop and business slogans

A tagline tells a customer in one line why they should walk in. These are sample lines for common small businesses. Use them to start, then put in your own promise, speciality or town.

Before printing a tagline on a board or packet, search online to make sure a competitor nearby, or a registered brand, is not already using the same words.

What a tagline is for

A slogan asks people to act. A tagline tells them what to expect. It sits under the shop's name on the board, the bill book, the carry bag, the visiting card and the WhatsApp Business profile, and it answers one question: why here and not next door? The sample lines on this page show the shape. Your own line must carry your own promise.

Write your tagline in four steps

  • Write down what regular customers already say about you: "always fresh", "never late", "explains everything", "fair rate". That is your real promise.
  • Add what makes you different: your town, your years in business, a family recipe, home delivery, a speciality.
  • Cut it to six words or fewer.
  • Read it to three customers. Keep the version they repeat back correctly.

Example: a sweet shop known for fresh jalebi every morning since the owner's grandfather's time might arrive at "Hot jalebi, every morning, since 1962". It is specific, it can be checked, and no other shop can copy it.

Honest and legal

  • Do not use "best", "number one", "cheapest" or "100% pure" unless you can prove it. Advertising rules in India expect claims to be true and provable.
  • Do not copy or closely imitate a known brand's line. Search online and ask around before printing.
  • Health and medical claims on food, cosmetics and supplements are regulated. Leave them out of a tagline.
  • If you want to protect a tagline you have built, a trademark attorney can advise on registration.

Where to put it

Under the name on the shop board, in letters about a third the size of the name. On the first line of your online profile. At the bottom of bills and messages. Use the same words everywhere for at least a year. A tagline becomes yours through repetition.

Using these slogans

  • Promise one thing you can keep every single day: fresh, on time, fair price.
  • Avoid "best" and "number one" unless you can prove it. "Fresh every morning" is stronger and safer.
  • Short enough for a shop board: six words or fewer.
