Environment and pollution slogans

World Environment Day is on 5 June and Earth Day on 22 April. These slogans work for both, and for any day a class is asked to make posters on pollution.

English

  • There is no second Earth. Look after the first.

  • Clean air is everyone's right and everyone's job.

  • The Earth is not ours to waste. It is ours to pass on.

  • Walk more. Smoke less. Breathe better.

  • Pollution has no borders. Neither should care.

  • Less honking, more hearing.

  • Small changes, repeated by millions, are big changes.

  • Share a ride. Spare the air.

  • Nature does not need us. We need nature.

  • Your street is part of the planet. Start there.

  • Carry your wrapper home. The planet is not your dustbin.

  • Walk when you can. The air will thank you.

  • The earth has no spare. Handle with care.

  • Small habits, clean planet.

  • Nature needs no holiday. It needs a break from us.

  • Be the neighbour the earth deserves.

Choosing a slogan for the day

Environment is a wide subject, and a slogan that tries to cover all of it says nothing. Before choosing, decide what the poster or rally is about this year: air, noise, waste, plastic, trees or water. If it is one of the last three, the pages for plastic, trees and water have sharper lines. The slogans on this page are for the wider message: one earth, shared by all, with nowhere else to go.

Write your own

Good environment slogans bring a huge idea down to the size of a hand. Not "stop pollution", but "walk to the shop". Not "protect nature", but "carry your wrapper home". Write the big idea, then ask: what can one student do about it before the evening? That answer is your slogan.

  • English pairs: earth and worth, green and seen, pollute and dilute, care and share, reuse and refuse.
  • Hindi pairs: धरती and भरती, प्रदूषण and भूषण, हवा and दवा, स्वच्छ and स्वस्थ, बचाओ and निभाओ.

Poster idea

Divide the chart down the middle. On one side draw the scene as it is: smoke, litter, a dry tree. On the other side draw the same scene put right. Write the slogan across the join, so that it belongs to both halves. Judges like a poster that shows a choice.

What to avoid

  • Frightening young children with the end of the world. Hope moves people more than fear does.
  • Scientific claims and figures you have not read in a textbook or an official source.
  • Using glitter, thermocol and plastic sheets on a poster about pollution.

Using these slogans

  • Pick one problem per poster: smoke, noise, litter or waste. One clear message is remembered.
