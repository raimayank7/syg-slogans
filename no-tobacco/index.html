No tobacco and anti-drug slogans

World No Tobacco Day is observed on 31 May, and 26 June is the International Day against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking. These slogans are for awareness rallies and posters.

If you or someone you know wants to quit, talk to a doctor. Slogans raise awareness. They are not treatment.

English

  • Say no the first time. It is the easiest time.

  • Tobacco takes more than it gives.

  • Choose your family over a habit.

  • Drugs promise a high and deliver a fall.

  • Real friends do not offer you a habit.

  • Addiction starts with "just once".

  • Quit today. Your body starts thanking you tomorrow.

  • No smoking: breathe easy, live fully.

  • Strong people ask for help.

  • My lungs, my game. No smoke.

  • The first "no" is the easiest. Say it.

  • Strong is the one who says "not for me".

  • Papa, please quit. I want you at my wedding.

  • Spend it on a football, not on a packet.

  • Asking for help is the brave part.

Where these slogans are used

World No Tobacco Day and anti-drug day rallies, school and college notice boards, NSS and NCC camps, village awareness marches and health centre walls. Young people are the main audience, so the tone matters more here than on any other page.

Write your own

Lectures do not work on teenagers, and slogans that only threaten are laughed at. The lines that land speak of freedom, strength, sport, money kept in the pocket, and the people who would be hurt. "My lungs, my game" says more to a fifteen year old than any warning. For younger children, slogans addressed to a parent or grandparent, asking them to stop, are simple and powerful.

  • English pairs: smoke and joke, quit and fit, choose and lose, breath and death, clean and keen.
  • Hindi pairs: नशा and दशा, तंबाकू and चाकू, छोड़ो and जोड़ो, धुआँ and कुआँ, जीवन and यौवन.
  • Punjabi pairs: ਨਸ਼ਾ and ਦਸ਼ਾ, ਜਵਾਨੀ and ਕਹਾਣੀ, ਛੱਡੋ and ਜੋੜੋ.

Getting help is part of the message

A slogan can open a conversation, but it cannot treat addiction. Good posters add one line saying where help is available: a doctor, the nearest government hospital or de-addiction centre, or a helpline number taken from an official source and checked on the day you make the poster. Speak of people who use tobacco or drugs with respect. They are someone's family, and shame keeps people away from help.

What to avoid

  • Medical claims and statistics you have not read in an official source.
  • Mocking or frightening pictures of patients.
  • Lines that blame a community, a state or a region.

Using these slogans

  • Slogans that speak of family and the future work better than slogans that only frighten.
