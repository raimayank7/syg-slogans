Science day and exhibition slogans

National Science Day is celebrated in India on 28 February, the day in 1928 when C. V. Raman announced the discovery of the Raman effect. These slogans suit exhibition stalls, charts and banners.

English

  • Ask why. Then ask how.

  • Science is curiosity with a notebook.

  • Test it. Do not just trust it.

  • Every expert was once a child with a question.

  • Wonder first. Measure next.

  • A failed experiment is a result too.

  • Think like a scientist: observe, question, check.

  • Facts first. Opinions after.

  • Science is not a subject. It is a way of looking.

  • Today's question is tomorrow's invention.

  • Be curious. It is free.

  • Ask why. Then ask it again.

  • Do not believe me. Test it.

  • The answer is good. The next question is better.

  • A wrong guess is still a step.

  • Look closer. It is more interesting than you think.

  • The lab is where "I think" becomes "I checked".

Where these slogans are used

Exhibition stalls, model labels, chart headings, the banner over the science lab door, quiz programmes and science club notice boards. On an exhibition day a slogan has about three seconds to stop a visitor who is walking past twenty stalls.

Write your own

Questions stop people. "Why is the sky blue?" above a stall does more than "Science is great". Write the question your model answers, and let the model be the reply. For a banner, the best lines praise curiosity and testing, not cleverness: ask, try, measure, check, try again.

  • English pairs: why and try, test and best, think and link, proof and truth, wonder and under.
  • Hindi pairs: विज्ञान and ज्ञान, प्रयोग and उपयोग, सवाल and कमाल, खोज and रोज़, तर्क and फ़र्क़.

For your stall

  • Heading: the question, in the largest letters.
  • Below it: one sentence saying what your model shows.
  • On a card: three steps of how it works, and what you would improve next time. Judges always ask the last one.

Keep the science right

A slogan on a science day must not say anything false. Avoid lines that claim science has proved a belief, cured everything or made anything "impossible". If you mention a scientist, an invention or a date, check it in your textbook first. The introduction above gives the reason for the date of National Science Day, which is a common quiz question.

What to avoid

  • Slogans that mock faith or tradition. Science day is for questions, not quarrels.
  • Copying the heading of another stall.

Using these slogans

  • For a model or stall, use a slogan that asks a question. Visitors stop for questions.
