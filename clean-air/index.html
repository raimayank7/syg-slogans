Clean air and air pollution slogans

Smoke from vehicles, burning waste, burning crop residue and fireworks all end up in the same place: the air everyone breathes. These slogans are for posters and campaigns that ask for cleaner habits.

English

  • The sky is not a chimney.

  • We all breathe the same air. Keep it worth breathing.

  • Short trip? Walk it.

  • Share the ride. Halve the smoke.

  • Engine off while you wait.

  • Do not burn leaves. Let them feed the soil.

  • A cycle has no exhaust pipe.

  • Blue skies are made by small habits.

  • Celebrate with light, not with smoke.

  • What goes up in smoke comes down in lungs.

  • Clean air is not a luxury. It is the first need.

  • Let children see stars, not smog.

Where these slogans are used

School poster competitions and eco-club campaigns, society notice boards in winter, "no burning" drives in villages and towns, car pool and cycle-to-school days, and appeals for quieter, cleaner festivals. The slogans on the wider environment page cover the earth as a whole. These keep to the air.

Write your own

Air is invisible, so show the reader something they can see: the grey sky, the dust on a leaf, a child's school mask, the chimney, the exhaust pipe, the heap of burning leaves at the corner. Then name the habit that helps. Walk or cycle short distances. Share the ride. Switch off the engine while waiting. Do not burn leaves and waste. Plant and protect trees. Slogans about one habit are believed. Slogans that only say "stop pollution" are not.

  • English pairs: air and care, smoke and choke, breathe and leave, clean and green, sky and why.
  • Hindi pairs: हवा and दवा, धुआँ and कुआँ, साँस and आस, आसमान and इंसान, जलाओ and बचाओ.
  • Punjabi pairs: ਹਵਾ and ਦਵਾ, ਧੂੰਆਂ and ਖੂਹ, ਸਾਹ and ਰਾਹ.

Speak to everyone, blame no one

Air pollution has many sources, and each group tends to point at another: city at village, village at city, drivers at factories. A good slogan asks the reader about the reader's own share. If your poster is about crop residue, write it with respect for farmers and mention alternatives only if you have learnt them from the agriculture department or university. If it is about fireworks, remember that festivals are dear to people, and ask for care, not for an end to celebration.

What to avoid

  • Health claims and pollution figures not taken from an official source you can name.
  • Advice on masks, purifiers or medicines. That is for doctors and health authorities.
  • Lines that single out any community's festival.

Using these slogans

  • A poster of one half grey and one half blue sky, with a child in the middle choosing, is simple and strong.
